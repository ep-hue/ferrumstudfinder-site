Do stud finder apps really work on an iPhone?

Short answer: yes, for metal. A stud finder app can find the steel screws or nails that hold drywall to a stud, and that is enough to locate the stud. It cannot see the wood itself, it cannot measure depth, and it cannot see copper or plastic. Below is how it works, what we measured with our own app, and when you should buy a real stud finder instead.

What the app is actually using

Every iPhone has a magnetometer. It is the sensor behind the compass, and it measures the magnetic field around the phone in microtesla (µT). Iron and steel bend that field. When the phone passes over a steel screw, the reading changes, and the app shows you the change.

That is the whole trick, and it explains both what works and what does not. There is no radar in a phone and no density sensor. An app that claims to "see" wood or pipes of any material through a wall is describing hardware the phone does not have.

What we measured

We build Ferrum: Stud Finder & Metal, so we tested it the way we would test any instrument: first at rest, then on a real wall.

  • At rest, after calibration: the reading stayed within 0.09 µT, with zero false alarms across 5,213 samples.
  • Over a screw in a wall: the reading peaked at 62.9 µT, about a hundred times the resting noise.
  • Repeatability: five passes over the same screw, five detections.

These are our numbers from our tests. Yours will differ with the phone model, the wall, how deep the screw sits and what else is nearby. The point is the gap: a screw near the surface is not a subtle signal.

Why an app can seem not to work

  • A magnetic case. MagSafe magnets and wallet cases are far stronger than a screw in a wall. Take the case off.
  • No calibration. The sensor always reads the Earth's field plus everything around it. A useful app zeroes that out first, so the number shows only the change.
  • Moving too fast. A screw head is small. Slide the phone slowly and flat against the wall.
  • Leading with the wrong end. The sensor sits near the top of the phone, around the camera area. That is the part that has to pass over the screw.
  • Walls full of metal. Plaster on metal lath, foil-backed insulation or steel framing give readings everywhere. A magnetometer is the wrong tool there.

A 60-second test for any stud finder app

  1. Remove the phone case.
  2. Hold the phone in the air, away from metal, and calibrate or zero the app.
  3. Slide the top of the phone slowly across a steel screw you can see, such as a door hinge screw.
  4. The reading should jump clearly over the screw and fall back when you move away. If it does not, the app or the setup is not usable on a wall either.

When to buy a real stud finder instead

A phone app is a good fit for hanging a shelf or a TV mount on an ordinary drywall-on-wood wall. Buy or borrow a dedicated tool when you need to find the edges of the stud rather than its fasteners, when the wall is plaster and lath, when you are looking for copper or plastic pipe, or when you are drilling where a mistake is dangerous. Quick answers

Do stud finder apps work on an iPhone?

Yes, for metal. An iPhone's magnetometer detects the steel screws or nails that hold drywall to a stud, which is how an app locates a wooden stud. It cannot detect the wood itself, copper, aluminum or plastic, and it cannot measure depth.

Can an iPhone find a wooden stud?

Indirectly. The phone cannot sense wood, but it can find the line of steel screws or nails driven into the stud. Several readings in a vertical line mean you have found the stud.

Why does my stud finder app show nothing?

The most common causes are a magnetic phone case, skipping calibration, moving too fast, or passing the wrong part of the phone over the wall. The magnetometer sits near the top of the iPhone, around the camera area.
